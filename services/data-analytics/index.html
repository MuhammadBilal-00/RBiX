Data Analytics & BI

Dashboards that turn scattered data into a single, daily-updated view of marketing performance, cash flow, and staff output — so you can answer the questions that actually run the business, not just the ones a monthly spreadsheet happens to cover.

The Problem

The numbers exist. They're just scattered across four different logins.

Owners of growing businesses usually can't answer simple questions fast: which marketing channel is actually producing bookings, not just clicks. Which branch or client is quietly becoming unprofitable. Which staff member's follow-up is actually converting versus just generating activity. These aren't hard questions in principle — but the data needed to answer them lives in an ad platform, a CRM, a POS system, and a spreadsheet someone updates once a month, and none of those systems talk to each other.

By the time someone finally pulls a report together — exporting from three places, reconciling the numbers, building the chart — the moment to act on it has already passed. The campaign already ran its budget, the unprofitable client already had another bad month, the underperforming rep already had another quiet quarter. The business isn't short on data. It's short on a single place to see it, updated often enough to matter.

Our Approach

We build the analytics layer on top of systems you already run.

No new platform for your team to learn — three steps that turn what's already being tracked into something you can actually read.

1

Tag everything at the source

Every ad, post, and channel gets tagged before it ever goes live, and that tag is carried all the way through to the final booking or sale — so attribution isn't a guess reconstructed after the fact.

2

Connect the systems that already hold the data

We wire into the CRM, accounting, and POS systems you already run — not a new tool for anyone to maintain, just a layer that reads what those systems already know.

3

Update daily, not monthly

The dashboard reflects what happened yesterday, not what happened last quarter — so a decision can be made this week instead of after the next scheduled report.

What's Included

Three dashboards, built on the data you already generate.

Each one is deployed on its own or as a stack, depending on which question is costing you the most right now.

Revenue & Marketing Attribution Dashboard

Tags every ad, post, and channel before launch, carries that tag through the CRM to the final booking or sale, and rolls it up by channel, campaign, and creative — updated daily — so the owner sees cost-per-lead and revenue-per-channel side by side, not weeks apart in two different reports.

  • Best for: any business running paid ads across multiple channels

Financial Health Dashboard

One view of cash flow, receivables aging, and profitability by branch, client, or service line, syncing automatically from accounting and POS systems and flagging anything trending the wrong way before it becomes a quarter-end surprise.

  • Best for: multi-branch businesses and practices needing real-time financial visibility

Staff / Agent Performance Analytics

Logs every interaction against the responsible staff member and calculates response time, conversion rate, and revenue generated automatically, producing a weekly leaderboard and individual scorecards grounded in real outcomes rather than impressions.

  • Best for: businesses with sales or service teams where performance varies by individual

Where the Data Comes From

This isn't a separate data-collection project.

It rides alongside AI & Automation at little to no extra cost, because the logging has usually already happened.

  • Every automated interaction is already being logged.The lead response engine, the appointment reminders, the invoice chaser, the customer conversation — each one writes a record the moment it happens, whether or not a dashboard exists yet to read it.
  • Data Analytics is what turns that log into something owner-readable.Instead of standing up a new tracking project from scratch, this service takes the exhaust already produced by your automations and CRM and rolls it into daily dashboards and scorecards.
  • That's why the cost stays low.The expensive part — instrumenting every conversation and transaction — is already done by the automation layer. Building the reporting on top of it is comparatively fast and cheap.
  • It's the retention layer.Automation makes the case for itself in dollars and hours once it's visible on a dashboard — this service is what proves, in numbers you can point to, that the automation is actually working.

Why It Matters

Decisions made on this week's numbers, not last quarter's.

  • Yesterday's numbers, not last quarter's.A daily-updated dashboard means decisions get made on what actually happened this week — not on a report that was already stale by the time someone finished building it.
  • Marketing spend gets justified or cut with real data.Cost-per-lead and revenue-per-channel, tracked to the actual booking, replace guesswork about which campaign is really working.
  • Underperforming branches and clients surface early.A branch, client, or service line quietly drifting unprofitable shows up on the dashboard weeks before it would show up in a monthly review.
  • Staff conversations happen with numbers, not impressions.Response time, conversion rate, and revenue generated are tracked automatically per person, so performance reviews start from facts instead of who happened to be memorable that month.
